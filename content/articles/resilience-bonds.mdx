A new class of resilience bonds is financing infrastructure projects across the island, structured so that investor returns are partly linked to measurable improvements in climate resilience rather than fixed independent of outcomes. The instrument is drawing interest from development finance institutions looking for a template that could be replicated across other small island states.

Proceeds are funding a mix of projects, from reinforced coastal roads to a distributed water storage system designed to reduce disruption after major storms. Officials involved in structuring the bonds say the resilience-linked design was necessary to attract the pricing needed to make the projects affordable for the national budget.

Analysts covering sovereign credit in the region say the approach, if it performs as designed, could improve Dominica's long-term credit profile by reducing the fiscal shock of future disasters, a dynamic that has weighed on the island's borrowing costs for years.
