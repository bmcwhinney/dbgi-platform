Dominica recorded 496,635 visitor arrivals in 2025, up from 432,989 in 2024, an increase of about 15 percent. The figures were presented by Marva Williams, chief executive of the Discover Dominica Authority and director of tourism, at the Caribbean Travel Marketplace in Antigua in May 2026, and reported by Caribbean Journal.

Stay-over arrivals rose 19 percent to 99,846, taking them above their 2019 level. The growth carried into this year: stay-over arrivals in the first quarter of 2026 were 10 percent higher than in the same period of 2025.

Cruise traffic grew faster still. The island received 409,761 cruise passengers between October 2025 and April 2026, 23 percent more than in the previous season and its strongest cruise performance since 2010-11.

The International Monetary Fund, concluding its annual review in May 2026, put tourism at 36 percent above pre-pandemic levels and named it, together with public investment, as a driver of the 4.5 percent growth in the economy in 2025.

## What the figures leave out

On the 2025 numbers, stay-over visitors were about one in five of all arrivals. The published figures count people, not spending, so they show volume rather than value, and they do not show how much of what visitors spend reaches local suppliers.

Two projects are meant to change the mix. The [international airport](/journal/dominica-international-airport-runway) under construction at Wesley is designed to bring long-haul flights direct to the island, and the [Boiling Lake cable car](/journal/cable-car-boiling-lake) is aimed at cruise passengers who have only a day ashore.
