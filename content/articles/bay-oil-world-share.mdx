Bay oil is distilled from the leaves of the West Indian bay tree. It is used to scent cosmetics, to make bay rum and in therapeutic products, and it is bought by perfume houses and health product makers in Europe and North America. In Dominica it is produced in the south and west, around Petite Savanne, Grand Bay and Mahaut.

It is often said that Dominica supplies most of the world's bay oil. The claim is worth reading closely, because it is one of the strongest statements made about any Dominican export.

## Where the number comes from

The most specific version is published by the Dominica Essential Oils and Spices Co-operative, which was established at Petite Savanne in 1964 and reports 564 active members. Its website states: "Annually, DEOSC distills over 22,000 liters of Bay Oil and exports 72% of our production, thereby meeting 75% of the global demand."

Three things follow from the wording. It is the producer's own figure, not an independent one. It carries no year. And it describes the share of world demand met by the co-operative's exports, which is not the same as Dominica's share of world production.

Other figures are in circulation. The reference site Dom767 says Dominica "once accounted for up to 70%" of the global supply of West Indian bay oil before Tropical Storm Erika in 2015, when a key distillery at Petite Savanne was destroyed and production fell sharply. Hurricane Maria in 2017 then damaged the network of small distillers, although the trees themselves recovered within about six months.

## What the arithmetic implies

Taken at face value, the co-operative's numbers describe a small market. Seventy-two percent of 22,000 litres is just under 16,000 litres of exports a year. If that meets three quarters of global demand, the whole world market is in the region of 21,000 litres. In a market that size, one co-operative can plausibly be the dominant supplier.

## What a buyer or investor would still need

Independent trade data on world bay oil volumes, the price per litre, the co-operative's output today compared with before the storms, and the scope for doing more of the processing on the island, in bay rum and finished products.
