Sea moss, the gelatinous red algae long boiled into a home remedy drink across the Caribbean, has become one of Dominica's fastest-growing export categories. What began as small-scale coastal farming for the local market has, over the past three years, turned into a genuine supply chain, with cooperatives coordinating harvests, processing, and export logistics for buyers as far away as the United Kingdom.

The shift tracks a broader wellness-industry demand curve. Sea moss gel and capsules have found a following in North American and European markets, and Dominica's warm, nutrient-rich coastal waters produce a crop that buyers describe as higher quality than competitors from other growing regions.

Local exporters say the challenge now is less about demand and more about standardising quality and scaling processing capacity without losing the traceability that lets them charge a premium. Several cooperatives have begun working with agribusiness advisors to formalise drying and packaging standards ahead of an anticipated certification push.

For a sector that barely existed as an export category five years ago, the growth has been abrupt enough that infrastructure, not demand, is now the binding constraint.
