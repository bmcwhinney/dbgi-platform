Sea moss has moved up Dominica's aquaculture agenda because of what farmers can earn from it, but the industry is still at an early stage. The clearest public account comes from the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, which reported in May 2024 on its work with three farming groups in Woodford Hill, Calibishie and Grand Bay.

The change that mattered was a switch of species. Farmers had been growing Gracilaria, which grows slowly, attracts weeds, takes a lot of labour to clean and dries down at about 18 kilograms wet to one kilogram dry. With training led by Thomas Nelson, a sea moss specialist from Saint Lucia, the groups moved to Eucheuma cottonii and began bringing in harvests of between 180 and 325 kilograms.

There are buyers. Two of Dominica's larger agro-processors purchase the raw product from local farmers. The Grand Bay group exports small quantities to the United States, and the Woodford Hill group is exploring cosmetics. "Dominica will soon be on the map for growing sea moss," Dorian Sanford, a government fisheries officer, told the FAO.

## What limits it

Weather is the main recorded constraint: Tropical Storm Bret disrupted farms in June 2023. The FAO account gives no figures for national production, export value or the prices farmers receive, and DBGI has not found more recent published data. Those are the numbers a buyer or investor would need, along with evidence that supply can be consistent in volume and quality from one season to the next.
