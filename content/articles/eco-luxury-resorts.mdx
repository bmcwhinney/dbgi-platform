Four eco-luxury resort projects are now in active development across the island, each built around the same wager: that travellers willing to pay premium rates increasingly want their destination's environmental credentials to be real, not just marketed.

The projects share a common design language, low-density construction, extensive use of local materials, and integration with existing forest and coastal reserves rather than clearing new land. Developers say this approach costs more per room to build but supports higher nightly rates and stronger repeat and referral business from a clientele that researches sustainability claims closely.

Government officials have signalled support for the eco-luxury positioning as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, the island's existing tourism base. Whether the model scales beyond four flagship properties will depend partly on how well the island can train and retain the specialised hospitality staff this tier requires.
