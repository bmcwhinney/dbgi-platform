The cable car being built from the Roseau Valley to the Boiling Lake is about 6.6 kilometres long, carried on 20 towers between a bottom and a top station. The ride is expected to take around 20 minutes. The walk it replaces is a hike of six hours or more through the Valley of Desolation, which puts the lake out of reach of anyone unable to make the climb and is hard to fit into a day in port.

The company says riders will see Trafalgar Falls, the Breakfast River gorge and the Valley of Desolation on the way up. The prime minister, Roosevelt Skerrit, has said the top station will have shops, restaurants and cafés.

The project is already an employer. In January 2025 its project manager, Randall Gliege, said just under 300 local workers were on site, drawn from across the island. By August 2025 the human resources manager, Gary Shillingford, put the workforce at more than 360, over 90 percent of them local.

## A moving finish line

The published completion dates have shifted with each report. In January 2025 the project was described as 60 percent complete and on track to launch at the end of 2025. In August 2025 the company's human resources manager said it was expected to be completed by February or March 2026, and a report the same month gave early 2026. As of early October 2026, DBGI has not found an announcement that the cable car is carrying passengers.

## The business questions

The reports reviewed for this article do not give a ticket price, the terms on which cruise lines will sell the ride, or how the shops and restaurants at the top station will be let. Those details will decide how much of the spending stays with Dominican businesses.

Demand is there to be tested. The island received 409,761 cruise passengers in the 2025-26 season, as [DBGI has reported](/journal/visitor-arrivals-2025). The open questions are how many of them the cable car can carry in a day in port, at what price, and what it means for the guides who lead the hike today.
