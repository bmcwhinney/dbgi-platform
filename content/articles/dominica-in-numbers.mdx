This briefing collects the headline figures behind DBGI's reporting. It is revised as new data is published.

## Growth and public finances

From the International Monetary Fund's annual review, concluded in May 2026:

- The economy grew 4.5 percent in 2025, up from 3.5 percent in 2024.
- Public debt stood at 103 percent of GDP in 2025, down from a post-pandemic peak of 118 percent. The regional benchmark is 60 percent.
- The primary deficit widened to 4 percent of GDP in the 2024/25 fiscal year.
- Inflation averaged 2.3 percent in 2025.
- The current account deficit was 38 percent of GDP in 2025.
- The risks the IMF names are spillovers from war, geopolitical and trade tensions, and natural disasters.

## Tourism

From the Discover Dominica Authority, presented in May 2026:

- 496,635 visitor arrivals in 2025, up from 432,989 in 2024.
- 99,846 stay-over arrivals in 2025, up 19 percent and above the 2019 level.
- 409,761 cruise passengers in the October 2025 to April 2026 season, up 23 percent.
- The IMF puts tourism at 36 percent above pre-pandemic levels.

Read more: [the 2025 visitor figures](/journal/visitor-arrivals-2025).

## Energy

- The 10 MW geothermal plant at Laudat entered commercial operation in August 2026.
- In June 2026 geothermal supplied about 43 percent of electricity and hydropower about 25 percent.
- The plant is expected to generate 59.6 gigawatt-hours in 2026, around half of national demand.

Read more: [the geothermal plant](/journal/hot-springs-powering-islands-ambitions).

## Infrastructure

- The international airport under construction at Wesley has a 3,000-metre runway. The government has described construction as on track for completion in 2027.
- The Boiling Lake cable car is about 6.6 kilometres long. No opening has been announced.

Read more: [the airport runway](/journal/dominica-international-airport-runway) and [the cable car](/journal/cable-car-boiling-lake).

## What the numbers do not yet show

No figure has been published for the effect of geothermal power on electricity tariffs. The tourism figures count visitors, not what they spend. DBGI has not found recent national data on export volumes for sea moss or cocoa.
