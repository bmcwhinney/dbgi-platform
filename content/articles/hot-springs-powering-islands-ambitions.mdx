Dominica's geothermal plant at Laudat, in the Roseau Valley, entered commercial operation in August 2026, according to ThinkGeoEnergy's report on the second-quarter results of its developer, Ormat Technologies. The 10-megawatt plant began feeding power into the national grid at the end of March, with full output targeted for April as commissioning continued. Exploration drilling began in 2011, so the project took about 15 years to go from first wells to commercial power.

The early numbers are large for a small grid. In June 2026, before commercial operation was declared, geothermal supplied about 43 percent of the island's electricity and hydropower another 25 percent, putting renewables at roughly two thirds of supply. The plant is expected to generate 59.6 gigawatt-hours in 2026, around half of national demand, rising to an estimated 60 percent of gross generation in 2027. The finance minister, Dr Irving McIntyre, has said this makes Dominica the first member of CARICOM to generate electricity from its own geothermal resources.

## How the deal is built

Ormat developed the plant under a 25-year power purchase agreement with Dominica Electricity Services (DOMLEC), signed in December 2023. Ownership passes to the government at the end of that term. The Caribbean Development Bank arranged a US$34.8 million financing package for the plant, backed by the Green Climate Fund and Canada, and the World Bank approved US$38.5 million in January 2024 for the transmission network, including a 33 kV underground line between Laudat and Fond Colé.

## What it means for business

The case for geothermal has always been that it replaces imported diesel with a local source whose cost does not move with the oil price. What none of the sources reviewed for this article gives is a figure for what the plant does to the tariffs that households and businesses pay. That depends on the price DOMLEC pays under the purchase agreement and on how it is passed through to customers. It is the number that matters most to the cold stores, agro-processors and hotels for whom electricity is a leading cost, and DBGI will report it when it is published.

The next steps reported are to investigate further geothermal prospects in the north of the island and to consider raising output from the Laudat reservoir.
